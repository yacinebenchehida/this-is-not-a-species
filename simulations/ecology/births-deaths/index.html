Population growth

Births and deaths

Can a population shrink even with endless food?

A population changes through births and deaths. A division adds one bacterium to the crowd. A death removes one. What matters is the difference.

Two sides of the same story

Animation in development

Count what joins. Count what leaves.

Change = births − deaths

Six divisions and six deaths leave the same number of living bacteria. The count is stable, but the individuals have changed.

A cell splitting in two counts as one net addition, not two.

Births > deaths

The population grows.

Births = deaths

The population stays stable.

Births < deaths

The population shrinks.

Food is only part of the story

Endless food does not guarantee a growing population. If deaths outnumber divisions, the crowd gets smaller.

Here, food is always available. We will change division and death separately to see what each one does.

Try this

  • Imagine six divisions and six deaths. Has nothing happened, or has only the total stayed the same?
  • Now imagine six divisions and nine deaths. How many bacteria has the population lost?

An illustrative model

Let N be the number of living bacteria. The letters b and d describe the average rates of division and death per bacterium, per unit of time. These are rates, not the total numbers of events.

dN/dt = (b − d)N

With constant rates, N(t) = N₀ exp[(b − d)t]. The population grows exponentially if b > d, stays constant if b = d, and declines exponentially if b < d.

This simple model has no arrivals or departures, no crowding and no food shortage. Its smooth curve describes an average; a small real population can fluctuate. The illustration above is static and does not calculate events.
