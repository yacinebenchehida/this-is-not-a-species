Population growth

The real growth curve

What happens if we leave a closed dish for longer?

Start with one supply of food and add no more. The population’s story can pass through four phases: getting ready, multiplying, levelling off and declining.

One closed dish. A longer story.

Animation in development

Food also keeps cells alive

Making new cells is only one use for nutrients. Living cells also need energy to maintain themselves. A closed reserve cannot support that forever.

The chart follows living bacteria. Dead cells may remain in the dish, but they are not counted as living.

  1. Lag phaseCells adapt. Little division does not mean no activity.
  2. Exponential phaseFood is plentiful. Births greatly exceed deaths.
  3. Stationary phaseNet growth is near zero. Births may balance deaths, or both may be rare.
  4. Death phaseDeaths exceed births. The living population declines.

A plateau is a moment in the story

Food shortage slows division. Prolonged starvation can then leave more cells dying than dividing. The stationary phase is not a promise that the population will stay there forever.

This is a typical closed-culture picture, not a timetable for every species. Some bacteria survive starvation for a long time. Waste and oxygen can matter too.

A little more

The future animation

One closed dish and one chart will follow the culture together. Coloured, labelled sections will show each phase as it happens. Food, divisions and deaths will come from the same simple model.

An illustrative model

The planned model will track living cells N and a finite nutrient reserve S. An adaptation period will delay rapid division. The division rate will depend on remaining food; cells will also consume food for maintenance. Mortality will rise under starvation.

Change in living cells = divisions − deaths
Food used = growth needs + maintenance needs

Deaths will not automatically put food back into the reserve. Feeding survivors would not revive dead cells. No new food enters during the closed-culture story.

The four phases will be an illustrative outcome, not a fit to a particular species or experiment. The static chart above is a drawn guide, not output from that future model.
